Provides the opportunity to listen to former slaves describe their lives. These interviews, conducted between 1932 and 1975, capture the recollections of twenty-three identifiable people born between 1823 and the early 1860s and known to have been slaves. The freed men and women discuss how they felt about slavery, slaveholders, how slaves were coerced; and about their families and freedom. They speak about living as African Americans during Reconstruction from 1865-1877 through the 1930s, and beyond. It is important to note that all of the interviewees spoke sixty or more years after the end of their enslavement. All known recordings of former slaves in the American Folklife Center archive from 10 collections are included in this online presentation together with transcriptions of the recordings. Includes biographies of many of the interviewers and a few interviewees; "Faces and Voices from the Presentation," a special presentation; and a section of Related Resources.  Several recordings were made on the South Carolina and Georgia coast of Gullah speakers. The collection includes twenty-eight songs and hymns (or song fragments) sung by the interviewees.

Correspondence, presidential letterbooks, diaries, speeches and messages, account and memorandum books, family papers, financial and legal records, printed matter, portraits, and other papers relating chiefly to Polk's political career in Tennessee and on the national level.  Documents struggles during the Andrew Jackson presidential administration over the Bank of the United States, nullification, and internal improvements as well as the annexation of Texas, war with Mexico, the Oregon question, and the acquisition of New Mexico and California.  Other subjects include slavery, tariff question, patronage and office seeking, plantation matters, and family affairs.  Includes 3 volumes of the papers of Polk's wife, Sarah Childress Polk. Correspondents include Alexander Anderson, Robert Armstrong, George Bancroft, Aaron V. Brown, James Buchanan, Edmund Burke, Lewis Cass, John Catron, J.F.H. Claiborne, George Mifflin Dallas, Andrew Jackson Donelson, John Fairfield, Ransom H. Gillet, Daniel Graham, Felix Grundy, Jeremiah George Harris, William H. Haywood, Henry Horn, Andrew Jackson, Cave Johnson, John K. Kane, Amos Kendall, Samuel H. Laughlin, William L. Marcy, John Y. Mason, Louis McLane, Ezekiel Polk McNeal, A.O.P. Nicholson, Gideon Johnson Pillow, J.G.M. Ramsey, Thomas Ritchie, Joel B. Sutherland, H.L. Turney, Martin Van Buren, Robert J. Walker, Harvey Magee Watterson, Levi Woodbury, Silas Wright, and Archibald Yell.
